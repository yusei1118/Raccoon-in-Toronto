## 📊 Overview
This project analyzes raccoon activity patterns in Toronto using SQL and Tableau.  
The goal is to identify where and when raccoon-related incidents occur, and what factors contribute to the damage.

---

## 🛠 Tools Used
- SQL (Google BigQuery)
- Tableau (Data Visualization)

---

## 🔍 Key Analysis

### 1. Spatial Analysis (Where is the most affected area?)
Using a map visualization in Tableau, the top 5 areas with the highest raccoon activity were identified.

- The results show that areas near High Park have the highest level of raccoon-related incidents.
- This suggests that proximity to green spaces and natural habitats plays a major role in raccoon activity.

---

### 2. Time-Based Analysis (When does it happen?)
Raccoon activity was analyzed across different time periods.

- Activity significantly increases between **8:00 PM and 4:00 AM**
- This confirms that raccoons are primarily **nocturnal** and more active when human activity is low.

---

### 3. Relationship Between Waste Damage and Raccoon Behavior
A comparison was made between:
- **Bins Compromised (waste damage)**
- **Raccoon Confidence Level (behavioral adaptation)**

Findings:
- There is a clear relationship between raccoon activity and waste damage.
- Although confidence levels did not vary significantly across categories, overall confidence was relatively high.
- This suggests that raccoons in urban areas are generally well-adapted to human environments and actively exploit accessible food sources.

---

### 4. Density Analysis (Incidents per km²)
To better understand the intensity of raccoon activity, incidents were normalized per square kilometer.

- **Toronto Center recorded 369 incidents/km²**
- The second highest area recorded **179 incidents/km²**

👉 This means Toronto Center has **2–3 times higher density** of raccoon incidents compared to other areas.

- This highlights how **urban density and human activity concentration** can amplify human-wildlife conflict.

---

## 📈 Key Insights

- Raccoon activity is **highly concentrated in specific areas**, especially near green spaces.
- Activity peaks during **nighttime hours**, aligning with raccoon behavior.
- **Waste accessibility is a major driver** of raccoon-related incidents.
- Urban raccoons show **consistently high confidence levels**, indicating strong adaptation to city environments.
- **High-density urban areas experience significantly more incidents per area**, not just in total numbers.

---

## 💡 Conclusion

This analysis demonstrates that raccoon-related issues in Toronto are driven by a combination of:
- Environmental factors (green spaces)
- Human behavior (waste management)
- Urban density

Improving waste management systems and focusing on high-risk areas could significantly reduce raccoon-related damage.
